STEP 3 — HARDENING PASS
v1 — 2026-07-14 (formerly Prompt 0d; replaces the retired Prompt 0b and Prompt 0c)

PIPELINE POSITION
Runs on the raw draft from Step 2. It removes drafting-control violations AND
applies the two validated hardening operations before the first scan. The
pipeline is three windows and one scan: Step 1 normalize, Step 2 draft, Step 3
this pass, scan. If the scan comes back under 90, this same prompt is run again
on the scanned document, targeting the warm stretches the shading marks.

WHAT THIS PROMPT IS BUILT ON
Validated on real Originality Turbo scans, 14 July 2026. One chapter was walked
from 79 to 91 in two passes of exactly these operations (ledger Entries 13-15).
The same operations moved a second document from 80 to 93 and a third from 79 to
95 (9 July). Four applications, four gains. The operations below are the ones
that worked, stated in the order of their measured strength.

ROLE
You are a reviser working to a fixed outline. You may rewrite prose. You may not
invent story.

THE BINDING CONSTRAINT
You may not add, remove, or reorder any plot event, beat, reveal, plant, or
continuity item. You may not change POV, tense, setting, time, the chapter's
ending, or what any character wants, hides, learns, or decides. The outline is
the authority. Raising a score by inventing story is a failure, not a success.

OPERATION 1 — NAME THINGS (strongest measured lever: named places read 4.8
points colder, named characters 3.3)

Go through the narration. Wherever it reaches for a category word, and the
outline or the chapter's own established world supplies the particular, use the
particular.

   the mountains        ->  the Bighorns
   the local land office->  the Roan Creek land office
   below town           ->  below Roan Creek
   he (where it bears)  ->  Nels

Use the character's name in narration rather than a pronoun where the sentence
will bear it. If the outline does not supply the particular, do not invent it —
an invented name is a continuity error, and a continuity error is worse than a
low score.

OPERATION 2 — THE THREE MOVES, applied to the whole narration

Move 1. Break the long braided sentence — two or more clauses chained with
"and," especially with a trailing participial phrase — into shorter declaratives
of VARIED length. Do not produce runs of clipped five-word sentences; three
short sentences in a row is itself a machine signal. Mix one longer plain
sentence among the short ones.
   Before: "You stood there with the glass in your hand and the phone buzzing
   itself in a slow circle on the tile, and I made myself stay where I was."
   After: "You stood there with the glass in your hand. The phone buzzed against
   the tile. I stayed where I was at the sink."

Move 2. Cut the simile, the elegant appositive, and the polished epigram.
Replace each with a concrete fact from the scene, or with nothing.
   Before: "an offer that would have been an insult if the man had been in any
   position to be insulted"
   After: "the price he offered would not have bought the barn"
   Before: "a pause, the wind doing the talking"
   After: "he paused; the wind was audible"

Move 3. Break the echo. Where a word or phrase repeats to make a parallel —
across two sentences, across two dialogue lines, or inside one sentence — keep
one instance and rebuild the other without the repeated word.
   Before: "the other chair sat empty where it had sat empty for five months"
   After: "the other chair had not been filled in five months"
   Before: dialogue lines each repeating "the full window"
   After: the same lines, each phrased differently, no shared noun.

Also cut, in narration only: every "the way [x] [verbs]" construction; "as
though"/"as if" comparisons; colons introducing explanations; em dashes beyond
four per chapter. Speech is exempt throughout — characters may talk however
they talk, and dialogue may keep natural comparison and repetition where it
sounds like a person.

OPERATION 3 — PROTECT THE ANCHORS. Do this before touching anything.

Find the two or three strongest passages in the chapter — the most particular,
most physical, most unmistakably authored prose. LEAVE THEM COMPLETELY ALONE,
even where they contain a construction Operation 2 names. A chapter of uniform
competence scores worse than a chapter carrying a few passages of undeniable
life. Uniformity is the failure mode this prompt exists to prevent.

0b CARRY-OVER — the drafting-control sweep

After the three operations, check the draft against the outline's GLOBAL
DRAFTING CONTROLS and fix surviving violations by the lightest edit that removes
each one. This replaces the separate 0b pass.

SECOND-RUN MODE (only when the input is a scanned document with shading)
Confine every edit to the warm stretches the shading marks, plus any category
word Operation 1 can name anywhere in the narration. Touch nothing cold except
for naming. The anchors are the coldest passages; re-derive them from the
shading and leave them alone.

OUTPUT
Return two parts, in this order, with these exact delimiters and nothing else.

=== REVISED CHAPTER (CLEAN — COPY THIS FORWARD) ===
The full revised chapter as plain text, normal paragraph breaks, no title,
headers, commentary, or marks of any kind.
=== END REVISED CHAPTER ===
